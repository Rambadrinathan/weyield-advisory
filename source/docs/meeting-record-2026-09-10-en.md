# MEETING RECORD
## WeYield — Strategic review of performance and business direction

**Date:** 10 September 2026
**Participants:** Emmanuel, Oli
**Subject:** Analysis of WeYield's financial performance and strategic direction (product positioning, market penetration, impact of AI)

## 1. Context

Since WeYield was founded, the company has seen steady growth in sales. The business is judged to be sound, intelligent, well-built and stable. However, looking at sales, costs and EBITDA together brings out **a point of concern that calls into question the financial valuation of the business as it stands.**

## 2. Finding no. 1 — EBITDA stagnating, even contracting, despite sales growth

- Despite a steady increase in sales since the company was founded, EBITDA is not moving — and over certain periods it is actually contracting.
- **Conclusion:** the financial value of the business is questionable, even though the business is in other respects sound and stable.
- **Underlying hypothesis:** the strength of the business — a strong product team and continuous product investment — may also be its weakness. The new features being developed do not demonstrate any evident return on investment.

## 3. Finding no. 2 — Market penetration is embryonic

- WeYield today serves roughly 120 customers, in a potential market estimated at between 600 and 1,000 customers.
- Market penetration is therefore all but embryonic (roughly 12 to 20% of the addressable potential).
- And yet the company's efforts remain directed at the product rather than at sales — **an imbalance to be corrected as a matter of priority.**

## 4. Finding no. 3 — Artificial intelligence: a threat and an opportunity

The business is judged to be highly disruptable by AI, which constitutes both a threat and an opportunity to be seized actively.

**Threat:**

- Some customers could be tempted to rebuild their own tool with AI and cancel their WeYield subscription.

**Opportunity — provided WeYield positions itself correctly:**

- Understand how the WeYield offering should be articulated in the face of a customer who develops an AI solution themselves, so that WeYield remains relevant within the customer's equation.
- Examine the case for a 100% migration to AI for WeYield itself — as every niche software company must now ask itself.
- Rebuilding the WeYield offering AI-natively would make it possible to deliver a different but at least equivalent solution, **in a fraction of the time and probably with a fraction of the team** that was needed to build the existing solutions.
- **Point to watch:** keep an eye on the cost of tokens, which becomes a cost line in its own right in an AI-native model.

## 5. An opportunity for strategic reinvention

Beyond cost reduction, an AI-native rebuild would open the way to a move up the value chain:

- Today WeYield addresses revenue managers, who are *operators*.
- By equipping WeYield with more strategic capabilities — fleet buying and selling, fleet distribution decisions by geographic zone — WeYield could move closer to the **owners** of the business and connect directly to its profitability.
- This would make it possible to propose new economic models, and to compare directly the profitability of an AI-run business against that of a traditionally run business.
- It would also open up a field of opportunity towards other profiles: fleet managers, and adjacent roles.

This reinvention will require a cultural reinvention of the company's DNA, but the opportunity looks worth the effort.

## 6. Conclusion and what is at stake next

- Rebalance investment between product and sales, in order to address the market penetration gap (120 customers out of 600–1,000 potential).
- Begin serious consideration of an AI-native migration of the WeYield offering, both as a **defensive** response to the threat of disruption and as an **offensive** lever for cost reduction and product acceleration.
- Explore extending the value proposition towards more strategic functions, connected to the customer's profitability, in order to widen the addressable market and change the type of counterpart at the customer.
- If these avenues are confirmed, WeYield would have both a way to **decouple costs from sales** (and therefore recover operating leverage on EBITDA) and new engines of growth — of a kind capable of feeding an ambitious business plan able to convince an investor of the current profitability and the future potential of the business.

## Translator's notes

Five points where the French carries weight the English flattens.

- **"opérateurs"** (§5) — Olivier means *executors*, people who carry out instructions, not "business operators". The contrast he is drawing is *opérateurs* versus *propriétaires* — the doers versus the owners. That single opposition is the pivot of his entire strategic argument, and it reads more sharply in French than the English word "operators" suggests.
- **"Constat"** (§2, §3, §4) — rendered here as "Finding". In French corporate writing it carries an auditorial, almost forensic weight: something established and no longer open to argument. Stronger than "observation", cooler than "problem".
- **"point de vigilance"** (§1) — the standard French corporate euphemism for a real problem. Softer than "red flag", considerably firmer than "something to note". He is being polite about something he considers serious.
- **"en valoir la chandelle"** (§5) — idiom, literally "to be worth the candle". Translated as "worth the effort". The register is measured, not enthusiastic — he is saying the returns justify the disruption, not that he is excited.
- **"Oli"** in the participants line is Olivier himself. The document is his own record of the session, not a third party's minutes.

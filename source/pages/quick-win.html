The Fleet Exposure Check

Ten questions. One page back. It tells an operator, in euros, what a five per cent move in used-car values would cost them — and compares it to their whole year's profit.

It needs no data partnership, no new integration, and no product. It uses public indices and answers the operator gives you.

And every submission answers the research question you cannot currently answer.

The move

Put the three together. A free diagnostic that gives the operator a number about their own business, and gives you the answer to kill-risk one — from the same ten questions.

The lead magnet is the market research. You are not choosing between validating the thesis and generating pipeline; the same form does both, across the whole addressable market rather than just your customers.

What the operator gets back

One page. No dashboard, no login, no jargon — for a buyer who runs the business like a craftsman and will not open a portal.

Every figure is arithmetic on what they told you plus a public index. Nothing here requires a residual data feed, because it measures exposure, not valuation.

1.3× your year A five per cent move in used-car values is worth more than everything the business earns in a year — and it is the one number nobody on your team tracks.

The three things moving it

Diesel, 58% of your at-risk fleet. Access rules are tightening in both your disposal markets, and the resale catchment for a non-compliant diesel shrinks every year. This is announced years ahead, which means it is the one you can plan around.

Electric, 11%. Used values move with new-car pricing you do not control. A manufacturer price cut passes through in four to eight weeks with no warning.

Timing. If you de-fleet in October, so does everyone else in your market. You sell into a trough you helped create.

Why this format

It speaks to the owner, not the revenue manager

Everything WeYield sells today is read by a revenue clerk. This is read by whoever signs the cheques — because it is denominated in the year's profit, not in RPD.

That is the “change the buyer” move from the strategy, executed in two weeks for the cost of a form, rather than in eighteen months for the cost of a product.

Why it lands with this buyer

One number, in their currency

A craftsman-entrepreneur who still runs an Excel macro will not read a dashboard and will not act on a percentage. “€145,000, which is 1.3 times your year” needs no abstraction at all.

And it is a number they cannot get anywhere else — not from their ERP, not from their accountant, not from a competitor.

The same ten answers do two jobs

This is what makes it worth doing before anything else. Every operator who fills the form hands you the exact dataset the fleet strategy is currently blocked on — and they hand it over willingly, because they get something for it.

Two weeks, using only what you already have

No residual data feed. No PMS integration. No new model. The hardest part is deciding the ten questions, and that is a conversation with a coach, not an engineering task.

  1. Decide the ten questionsWith a coach, in one sitting. They must be answerable from memory by an owner on a phone — no lookups, no exports. If a question needs the ERP, it is the wrong question.
  2. Write the exposure calculationFleet × at-risk share × average disposal value × the move. Public indices for the powertrain and market adjustments. This is a spreadsheet before it is code, and the coach should be able to check it by hand.
  3. Build the form and the reportA page on weyield.io, and a one-page PDF. Your own model writes the three narrative paragraphs; everything numeric is computed. Same discipline as the brief: the model writes, it never calculates.
  4. Test on ten existing customersPeople who will tell you if the number is wrong. This is also the moment you find out whether the ten questions are answerable — and it doubles as the kill-risk validation you owe yourself anyway.
  5. Publish it, and put it through the four channels you already runNothing new to build here — see below.
Distribution — all four already exist

The market-trends product. Already free, already gated behind a form, already has an audience that expects something from you. Add this alongside it.

ERP partner introductions. You already contact their clients on their behalf. This is a better thing to arrive with than a product pitch — and it makes the partner look good.

LinkedIn. Already being scraped for contacts. Now there is a reason to write.

Emmanuel's own platform. Conferences, the reputation, twenty years of being the name people know. “Come and find out what your fleet exposure is” is a better talk than any product demo.

What could go wrong
  • The number is wrong for someone. Mitigate by testing on ten customers first, and by stating the method on the page — this is a diagnostic, not a valuation, and it should say so.
  • Owners cannot answer the ten questions. That is itself a finding, and a serious one: it would mean the fleet product has a data problem before it has a product problem.
  • It cannibalises the paid conversation. Unlikely — it deliberately stops at exposure. What it cannot tell them is when to sell, which is the product.

Where it sits in the plan

It does not replace anything. It brings forward the two things that were furthest away — the owner as a buyer, and the residual thesis — and pays for the research at the same time.

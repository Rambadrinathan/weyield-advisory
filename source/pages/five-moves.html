WeYield · AI-native product & GTM portfolio · Sept 2026

Decision-rich,
judgement-poor.

A 300-car operator makes the same decisions as Hertz. Hertz has a department for each one. The operator has their gut, one clerk, and a spreadsheet.

AI does not fix this by forecasting better — Revenue Horizon already forecasts.

It fixes it by putting expert judgement at the point of every decision, in the operator's language, on their channel, at near-zero marginal cost. The department without the headcount.

WeYield owns the ingredients: cross-tenant data, an encoded method, the integrations, a coaching culture. Five moves put that judgement where it is missing.

The problem, drawn

Five decisions run a car rental business. They differ by a factor of a thousand in frequency, and every one of them is made late, on partial data, by someone with forty other things to do.

Read down each column: who makes that call at a major, who makes it at a long-tail operator, and whether WeYield is in the room.

Five moves, and what each one changes

Two of them raise revenue per account. Two change how many accounts WeYield can reach — solving "two salespeople, 600 operators" without hiring a rep. One is the engine the others run on.

Bars show build-and-land windows, not effort. Network Edition starts on Monday and lands last, because franchisor procurement is the long pole.

Monday Brief

Decision
The weekly one — and it is the only column where an owner currently gets nothing at all in writing.
What it is
Every Monday, the owner gets a 500-word brief in a coach's voice: what moved, what the market did, what is coming, and three actions each with a euro value. They reply DONE, SKIP or WHY.
Why now
Uses only data WeYield already holds. No partnership, no new integration, no user interface — tier-A briefs land as drafts in the coach's own mailbox.
The catch
It is also the engine. Facts builder, rules engine, composer and verifier are shared by everything below.
GateDoes Performance Hub expose per-account data at station × car group × day grain? If not, week 1 is a data-access sprint and the plan slips two weeks — not eight.

Managed Revenue RM-as-a-Service

Decision
The daily rate — for the operators who have nobody to make it.
Problem
Most of the long tail cannot hire a revenue manager: €40–60K loaded, scarce skill, and a 200-car fleet does not justify it. Rates get set by copying the neighbour. WeYield's software needs a user who does not exist in the building.
Product
WeYield runs your revenue function. Guardrails set at onboarding — floors, ceilings, maximum daily move, channels. The agent acts inside them; anything outside goes to a coach.
Sold as
A salary substitute, not a tool. “A revenue manager for €2,000 a month instead of €4,500.” Or base plus a share of RPD uplift — the Aircar +6% is the proof point.
€75Kvalue of +3% RPD to a 300-car operator, per year
€600Kone coach × 25 accounts × €24K, at ~88% gross margin
RiskLiability for autonomous moves. Start at recommend + one-tap approve; graduate to execute within guardrails per account, only as that account's telemetry earns it.

Raises revenue per account · month 3–6

Ancillary Yield the counter moment

Decision
The counter moment — thousands of times a day, by whoever is on shift.
Problem
Upgrades, waivers, GPS, child seats, fuel, additional driver, cross-border are 25–35% of revenue at 80–90% margin — priced flat, sold inconsistently, by high-turnover staff. WeYield yields the daily rate to the euro and leaves the highest-margin third of the business unmanaged.
Why now
ML prices the upsell per reservation; a language model writes the script for this booking in the agent's language — “compact, 9 days, two adults: offer the intermediate at +€9 a day, here is the sentence.” Plus a pre-arrival offer 48 hours out — the airline pattern that converts 8–15%.
Sold as
Pure upsell to the base. Buyer is the RM and the COO — already a persona on WeYield's own site. Outcome-priceable from day one, because the metric is clean.
Scope disciplineNeeds a counter touchpoint, which WeYield does not own. Ship pre-arrival email and price write-back first — no counter integration required. Leave the handheld screen to the PMS partners.

Network Edition one deal, 200 operators

Decision
WeYield's own — how two salespeople reach 480 operators they do not have.
Problem
WeYield sells one operator at a time, at ~7 logos per rep per year. Meanwhile franchisors run field-support teams that cannot scale across 50–200 franchisees, and royalties are a percentage of revenue — so network RPD is their P&L, not a nice-to-have. Consultants cap out at about eight clients.
Product
A franchisor console: Monday network brief, league table, benchmark cells, underperformer alerts. Franchisee briefs carry the network's branding. A consultant workspace with per-client drafts.
Why WeYield
It already serves franchisees of several brands. A cross-brand benchmark is the one thing no franchisor can build — each sees only its own network.
SequencingFranchisor procurement runs 9–18 months, so open it Monday. The consultant channel converts in weeks and is the fast first step — and a pilot with one franchisor regional division beats a pitch to the group.

Raises number of accounts · month 4–8

WeYield Start agent-onboarded, self-serve

Decision
Where WeYield's addressable market stops — and why it stops there.
Problem
Implementation — schema mapping, car groups, seasons, stations, training — costs more than a small operator's whole contract. So the market is quoted as 600–1,000, while thousands of 20–150 car operators exist in islands, resort towns and emerging markets. Exactly where WeYield already has a footprint.
Why now
An onboarding agent reads a messy PMS export, maps it to the schema, proposes car groups and seasons, asks three questions, and is live in an hour. The Academy becomes an in-product tutor. Support is AI-first. Zero human implementation.
Lead magnet
A free AI Visibility Audit — “here is how you appear when a traveller asks an assistant for a car at your airport.” Nobody else can offer it, and it is a rate-shopping problem, which is what WeYield already is.
RiskSupport load. Strict scope, AI-first support, community forum. Cannibalisation is negligible — this is a segment that has never bought, not a discount for one that has.

Two I considered and set aside

Listing what was rejected, and why, is what makes the other five a portfolio rather than a brainstorm.

Damage & claims agent

The second-largest cost line, and vision models make photo-based assessment genuinely real now.

But it sits outside WeYield's pricing-and-data core, needs counter workflow and photo capture WeYield does not own, and specialist vendors already exist. Partner, do not build.

Better forecasting

Not the constraint. Gradient-boosted models already do this well, language models will not beat them at it, and Revenue Horizon exists.

Improving forecast accuracy a few points changes nothing an owner can feel. The gap is judgement, not prediction.

How the five fit

Two expand the contract. Two expand the reach. One is the engine all four run on — which is the entire argument for building it first.

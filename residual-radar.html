The car is the asset. The rental is just the carry.

Revenue management optimises what a car earns while you own it. That number pays the clerks, the airport concession and the wash bay.

The year's actual result is set on the day the car is sold — against a depreciation figure someone estimated two years earlier.

No vendor sells that decision to this segment. WeYield holds two of the three things needed to.

How the buy-versus-sell model works

A residual curve is not a straight line, and that is the entire opportunity. Value falls in a smooth glide punctuated by cliffs — dated, knowable events that knock several points off in a week.

Hold past a cliff and you pay for it. The decision is not whether to sell but which side of which cliff, and no long-tail operator has the curve in front of them.

The signal only WeYield can compute

In a seasonal market every rental operator de-fleets in the same weeks. They flood their own local used market and sell into the trough they collectively created.

No operator can see the wave — they see only their own cars. Autovista and Indicata cannot see it either; it hasn't reached the market yet. WeYield can see it forming, months ahead, across its own customer base.

Diesel and electricity are not the same kind of risk

Both get called “powertrain risk” and lumped together. They behave completely differently — different speed, different signal, different response — and confusing them is how a fleet plan goes wrong.

One is a policy cliff announced years ahead. The other is a price shock with no warning at all.

What actually moves fleet value — and what does not

Ranked for a Mediterranean franchisee: 1,000 cars, 60% diesel, 20% BEV, 20% petrol, €14M disposal pool, 24-month hold. Annual net profit for scale: €336K.

Diesel — the mechanism

It is not the fuel price. It is whether the car can enter the city.

  • Euro standard vs zone rules decide the resale catchment. A Euro 6b that cannot enter Paris, Brussels or Milan is sold to a smaller, poorer pool — or exported at a discount.
  • The announcements are public and dated. A 2029 restriction announced in 2026 is a priceable event three years early. This is unusually forecastable for a residual driver.
  • Export demand is the floor — North Africa, the Caucasus, Eastern Europe. It moves with FX and with those markets' own emission rules.
  • Pump-price spread between diesel and petrol matters, but it is second order behind access rules.
Electric — the mechanism

A used EV competes with a new one, and new EV prices move overnight.

  • New-price cuts pass through at close to 1:1 within 4–8 weeks, decaying with age. This is the dominant driver and the fastest.
  • Subsidy changes cut both ways. A new-car purchase subsidy depresses used values by making new cheaper — a counter-intuitive result worth modelling explicitly.
  • Battery State of Health is becoming a priced attribute. A certificate (AVILOO, TÜV and similar) is starting to be worth real money at disposal.
  • Electricity price works only through total cost of ownership: expensive power narrows the running-cost advantage and softens used demand. Real, but small next to the above.
Be honest about what the product can and cannot do

WeYield cannot forecast diesel policy or a manufacturer's pricing decision, and should never claim to. What it can do is tell an operator what their exposure is, alert them the day a driver moves, and say what to do about it.

Exposure, alerting and response is a complete and saleable product. Claiming macro forecasting is the black-box-distrust trap all over again — in a market that has already named distrust as its adoption blocker.

Where the data comes from

Four tiers. Two are free and available on Monday, one is a scraping job WeYield already has the machinery for, and one is a commercial negotiation that has to start now because it is the long pole.

The fifth input is the one nobody can buy — and it is why this becomes a moat rather than a resale of somebody else's feed.

The negotiating position worth opening with

Do not open as a procurement conversation. Open as a barter. Indicata and Autovista genuinely want rental de-fleet transaction data in secondary and island markets, which is where their coverage is thinnest and where WeYield has customers.

Data-for-data, or a heavily reduced licence with contributed data, is a realistic structure — and it is far cheaper than buying redistribution rights, which is the expensive part and the one that takes months, because the whole point is to show the numbers to 120+ operators.

What to do about it, in order

Steps 1 to 3 cost nothing and can start this week. Step 4 is the long pole and is why the conversation opens now rather than when the module is designed.

  1. Instrument disposals immediately — before anything else.Every unit WeYield's customers sell from today: date, vehicle, spec, mileage, channel, price achieved. This is the proprietary asset, it costs almost nothing to start capturing, and it cannot be backfilled. Six months of delay is six months of the moat never built.
  2. Build the fleet ledger from data you already have.Acquisition date and price, in-service date, mileage, planned disposal. Kill-risk 2 from the strategy applies here: confirm which of these fields actually flow from the PMS integrations before promising a timeline.
  3. Wire the free signals and ship the exposure view.The LEZ registry, the oil bulletin, ENTSO-E and OEM list prices are free and available now. On their own they give you an exposure report — “61% of your fleet is diesel, and 40% of it cannot enter your two largest resale cities from 2029” — which is a sellable artefact before a single valuation licence is signed.
  4. Open the Indicata conversation as a barter.Critical path for everything above. Commercial negotiation runs longer than any integration, so it starts in week one of Horizon 2, not when the module is designed.
  5. Fit the coefficients; do not assume them.Powertrain factors, LEZ elasticity and EV pass-through can all be estimated from 2022–2026 history, which contains a real energy shock and a real EV price war to learn from. A fitted coefficient is defensible to a customer; a rule of thumb is not.
  6. Ship the de-fleet wave first among the paid features.It needs no valuation licence — only WeYield's own forward plans — it is worth real money on its own, and it is the one thing a competitor cannot copy by buying the same feed.

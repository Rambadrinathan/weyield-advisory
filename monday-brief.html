Reasoning contract

Every number carries a reference

The model never computes. A deterministic facts pack supplies each figure; the PH-pace-w41 tags point at it. A verifier matches every numeric token to a fact before send — or drops the sentence.

Rules, not prompts

Actions come from a rules engine the coach owns

Ten rules to start. The model ranks and explains candidates; it cannot invent one. New actions are added by adding rules, with the coach — not by prompting.

Owner's decision

The Toulon line is the first step up the value chain

Fleet size at a station is the owner's call, surfaced by revenue data WeYield already holds. No residual data needed yet — that is Horizon 2.

The reply is the Analyst

DONE and SKIP are telemetry. WHY is a conversation.

A WHY gets an answer in the thread within two minutes, grounded in the same facts and a bounded set of drill queries. This is the conversational analyst — with a narrower, safer surface than a chat box.

Cadence and channels

  • Monday 06:30 local to the account's head office. Daily for top accounts is a Horizon 2 option.
  • Email by default; WhatsApp Business where owners read that way (islands, North Africa — verify by region); PDF attached for forwarding.
  • Six languages from day one. The facts pack is language-neutral; the Composer writes natively.

The coach in the loop

  • Tier A (top ~20 by ACV): the brief is created as a draft in the coach's own mailbox. Five minutes to edit and send under their name. No review UI to build.
  • Tier B/C: auto-sent, signed “Your WeYield coaching team”. A daily digest to the coaching lead.
  • Anything the verifier flags is held for a coach regardless of tier. Coach edits are captured as training signal.

The reply loop — the Analyst, narrowed

  • DONE / SKIP / MODIFIED n → recorded against the action. One-line acknowledgement.
  • WHY n → the Analyst answers in-thread within two minutes, grounded in the same facts pack, using a bounded set of drill templates — parameterised queries by station, group, window, channel. Never free-form SQL. Every figure verified the same way as the brief.
  • Out-of-scope questions get an honest “that is outside this week's pack — I'll flag it to your coach” and a ticket.
  • A quiet week is a valid brief. If no rule fires, the actions block reads: “No action recommended this week — pace and rates are where they should be.” Manufacturing actions to look useful is the failure mode.

The reasoning contract

Product rules, not engineering preferences. They are what make the Brief sellable to an industry whose stated adoption blocker is black-box distrust.

  1. The model never computes. Every number in prose comes from the facts pack, built deterministically. The Composer is forbidden — in the prompt and by the verifier — to add, subtract, percentage or estimate.
  2. Every claim carries a reference. Each sentence in the structured output carries refs: [] pointing at fact IDs. A sentence with a number and no reference is dropped before send.
  3. Actions come from rules, not the model. The Composer ranks, selects up to three, and explains. It cannot originate an action the rules did not propose.
  4. The Brief recommends; the human executes. v1 writes no rates and moves no cars. Pricing Insights write-back remains the execution path, triggered by a person.
  5. Competitor information is what the customer already sees in Market Radar — nothing more.
  6. Benchmarks are opt-in and k-anonymous. Shown only if the account opted in and the peer set has at least five operators. Indices, never absolute rates.
  7. Confidence is stated, and hedged where the forecast is the basis.
  8. Missing data renders as missing. Never as zero. The Monaco line in the sample is the pattern.
  9. A quiet week is a valid brief. Zero actions is an allowed and expected output.
  10. The verifier is a hard gate. Numeric fidelity below 100% holds the brief for a coach. There is no “mostly right” send.

Architecture — deterministic core, generative surface

The model does two things: write, and answer. Everything else is code. That is the same shape the Decision Feed will have.

  1. Facts Builder deterministic
    SQL/Python, per account per week → facts.json: KPIs, movers, pace, competitors, forecast, fleet, benchmark, data quality. Every fact has an ID, a source and a link.
  2. Rules Engine deterministic
    Methodology encoded as rules; each fires with evidence and a value estimate → candidates.json. Coach owns the rules; engineering owns the runtime.
  3. Composer model
    Frozen, cached system prompt: coach persona, style guide with real exemplars, the reasoning contract, output schema, language policy. Volatile inputs after the cache breakpoint: facts, candidates, account profile, the last four briefs, telemetry summary. Structured output → brief.json.
  4. Verifier deterministic + judge
    (a) Every numeric token ↔ a fact value in its refs; every candidate ID exists; values match. Fail → drop sentence → regenerate once → hold for coach. (b) LLM judge on a 10% sample: action logic, tone, brevity.
  5. Renderer deterministic
    Email HTML · WhatsApp text · PDF · coach draft. The numbers table is rendered from facts, never generated.
  6. Delivery deterministic
    Tier A → draft in the coach's mailbox. Tier B/C → auto-send. Digest to the coaching lead. Weekly generation runs through the Batch API at half price.
  7. Reply Handler model on WHY
    DONE / SKIP / MODIFIED parsed deterministically → telemetry. WHY and free text → the Analyst over facts + 6–10 drill templates, streamed, verified, in-thread.
  8. Telemetry deterministic
    briefs · brief_actions · action_responses · outcomes (T+7, T+28 on the action's scope vs forecast baseline) · analyst_turns. Outcomes is the point: in six months it says which rules make money, for whom, at what confidence. It cannot be backfilled.

Data residency

The facts pack holds no traveller personal data — aggregates, rates, counts, forecasts. Recipients are merged at render time. The provider is a sub-processor for business-confidential data and is listed as such in the DPA. Run inference in an EU region (Claude on Bedrock EU or Vertex eu, or first-party inference-geography controls) and confirm zero-data-retention terms in week 1. Benchmark aggregates: k ≥ 5, index form, opt-in, reviewed once by counsel against existing customer terms.

Acceptance criteria and evals

Hard gates

  • Numeric fidelity 100% on replay and on every production brief
  • Zero invented figures in production in the first 90 days
  • Every action traceable to a rule and a candidate ID
  • Missing data rendered as missing — never a zero for an unknown

Quality, week 8

  • Coach rubric ≥ 4.0 / 5 on 30 sampled briefs
  • Tier-A coach edit time ≤ 5 minutes
  • ≥ 25% of briefs receive a reply; ≥ 1 action DONE per account per month
  • Open rate ≥ 60% among named recipients

Economics, week 8

  • Cost per brief ≤ €0.30 all-in
  • Package B trial-to-paid ≥ 30% — or a clear reason to bundle instead

Evals, built week 2, run on every change

  • Replay set: 30 historical account-weeks → fidelity, ref coverage, action validity
  • Adversarial set: gaps, contradictions, a quiet week, a data-quality flag — degrade gracefully, say nothing when there is nothing
  • Language set: same facts in six languages — numbers identical, prose native

What it unlocks

This is Horizon 1 of the strategy in a single product.

  • Methodology encoded — the rule set is the written-down coaching method, and it grows weekly.
  • The loop instrumented — outcomes is the evidence base for outcome pricing and the Decision Feed's learning.
  • The Analyst shipped — as the reply channel, with a narrower and safer surface than a chat box.
  • The owner reached — with data WeYield already holds. The Toulon fleet-size line is the first fleet conversation.
  • Cost-to-serve collapsed — the 100 accounts that get no coach note today get one every Monday for €0.20.
  • The Decision Feed becomes the Brief at daily cadence, guardrails per operator, execution via Pricing Insights write-back, learning from outcomes. Same builder, rules, composer, verifier.
  • The Fleet P&L becomes new fact types (acquisition cost, residual index from Indicata) and new rules (R11+: de-fleet timing). The brief already has the slot.

Open questions for Emmanuel

The assumptions this spec rests on. Number 1 decides the timeline.

  1. Data access. Does Performance Hub expose a per-account API or warehouse at station × car group × day grain for RPD, utilisation, bookings, pace, fleet counts and idle days?
  2. Coach notes today. Do coaches write weekly notes now — how many accounts, in what form? These are the style exemplars.
  3. Coverage. How many accounts run all three of Market Radar, Performance Hub and Revenue Horizon?
  4. Languages. Which six cover 90% of recipients?
  5. Contracts. Do current terms permit an LLM sub-processor and opt-in aggregate benchmarking? Who reviews?
  6. Channels by region. Where do owners read WhatsApp rather than email?
  7. Tiering. The top-20 accounts by ACV — the same list the repricing exercise needs.
  8. Fleet fields. Do idle-unit counts and station fleet plans already flow into Performance Hub, as the site implies? Rule R3 depends on it.
